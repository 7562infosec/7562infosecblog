If Q3 2026 had a theme, it was that attackers kept finding the system we hadn't inventoried, and they were already inside it by the time we heard about it.

**ShinyHunters ran the longest story of the quarter.** The Oracle PeopleSoft zero-day, CVE-2026-35273, was exploited from June. Mandiant and GTIG didn't describe the mass exploitation until September 25, so a lot of teams patched into a compromise already in progress. The group also breached the FBI's own recruiting site in late September, and the FBI says ShinyHunters has extorted more than $70 million this year. A Dutch arrest on September 15, an FBI arrest announcement on September 29, and a leak site that went dark October 1 ended the storefront. They did not end the access or the stolen data. For any internet-facing ERP or HR platform, treat the patch date as the start of a compromise assessment, and hunt back to June.

**Ransomware volume hit a new high, and Clop came back.** Comparitech counted 2,627 attacks in Q3, about 29 a day, up 29% on Q2 and 61% on last year. Qilin posted 357 claims and The Gentlemen 342. Clop went from one claim in Q2 to 48, riding a PTC Windchill and FlexPLM flaw, CVE-2026-12569, with Harley-Davidson listed September 10. Trackers disagree on monthly totals, and only 247 of those 2,627 attacks were confirmed by the victim. The direction is not in dispute. Product lifecycle and file-transfer platforms are usually owned by engineering, not security, and Clop knows it. Get them into your asset inventory and exposure scans, and plan your comms for the gap between "claimed" and "confirmed."

**Edge devices got a three-day clock.** On September 9, CISA added a Cisco authentication bypass, CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0), along with Citrix and Fortinet entries, and set a federal deadline of September 12. Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-19490 (CVSS 9.3) was reportedly exploited from September 3, before most advisories landed. The advisory is a lagging indicator. Set your patch SLA for KEV-listed, internet-facing devices in hours, and keep logs long enough to look back past the disclosure date.

**Third parties carried the financial-sector risk.** EY was reportedly hit through a third-party platform in July. TripleX claimed roughly a terabyte from Bank of Baroda. Greenberg Traurig notified 150,000 people after a Silent Ransom Group attack in August. On September 24, Bitget lost about $388 million through a zero-day in a third-party security product. A security tool with admin reach is part of your attack surface, not a control sitting outside it. Ask every vendor what of yours they hold, for how long, and what privileges their tooling has in your environment.

**The takeaway for Q4: measure how far back you can look, not only how fast you patch.** PeopleSoft was under attack for months before the advisory. The Pentagon's DMDC data was accessed from October 2025 until it was discovered July 16, 2026. The common thread in this quarter was attackers who were already inside. If your telemetry and retention can't answer "what happened here six months ago," your patch speed won't save you.
